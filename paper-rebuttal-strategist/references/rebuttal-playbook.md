# Rebuttal Playbook

## High-Yield Response Patterns

### Misunderstanding

Structure:

- acknowledge why the wording caused confusion
- quote or point to the exact paper location
- restate the corrected interpretation
- promise a concrete revision if allowed

Avoid saying the reviewer "missed" something.

### Missing Experiment

Structure:

- state whether the requested experiment is central or diagnostic
- provide existing evidence closest to the request
- give new results only if actually run
- if not run, explain feasibility and planned revision without pretending it is done

### Novelty Concern

Structure:

- separate overlap from difference
- identify the technical object that changes
- compare assumptions, inputs, outputs, and evidence
- add citation or wording fixes if the paper overstated novelty

### Reproducibility Concern

Structure:

- state what artifacts are available
- state what they reproduce exactly
- state what is not included and why
- commit to specific cleanup if allowed

### Theory Or Math Concern

Structure:

- concede notation or proof gaps if real
- give the corrected assumption or weaker theorem
- explain whether the empirical claim depends on the theorem
- avoid broad claims like "the proof is straightforward" without showing the key step

## Tone Rules

- Be precise before being persuasive.
- Concede small valid points early.
- Do not use rebuttal space to repeat the abstract.
- Do not answer a harsh review with harshness.
- Avoid "we believe"; prefer "Table 2 shows", "Appendix C defines", or "we will revise".

## Final Check

Before finalizing, ask:

- Does each paragraph target a decision-relevant belief?
- Is any claim unsupported by current evidence?
- Did we spend too much space on a reviewer who is already positive?
- Did we concede the real limitation without making the paper look weaker than necessary?
